Sonal Bhatia

Mechanical & Aerospace Engineering at Princeton University. Minors in Computer Science and Robotics.

Princeton, NJ

I’m a senior at Princeton studying Mechanical & Aerospace Engineering, with minors in Computer Science and Robotics. I work at the intersection of control theory, machine learning, and hardware, and I’m drawn to problems that refuse to stay inside one discipline: a filtering problem that turns out to be a learning problem, or a model that only matters once it runs fast on real silicon.

At my core, I’m a problem-solver who likes being deployed where the work actually happens. I want to sit close to the people and systems with the problem, find the constraint that really matters, whether that’s a latency target, a power budget, or a robot tumbling with no gravity to lean on, and then build whatever it takes to get past it, from the math to the code to the hardware.

Right now I’m building microgravity inspection robots in Prof. Radhika Nagpal’s lab and working on physics-informed neural networks with Prof. Ryne Beeson. Before that, I spent two internships at Apple on AI performance. More in my CV.

selected publications

  1. Thesis
    Multi-Particle Control for Nonlinear Data Assimilation in Chaotic Systems
    Sonal Bhatia
    Apr 2026
    Advisor: Prof. Ryne Beeson. Reader: Prof. Christine Allen-Blanchette. 189 pages.
    Abs Bib

    An imitation-learning framework that amortizes the cost of optimal particle-filter nudging offline. A Feynman-Kac Monte Carlo oracle generates expert controls across a low-discrepancy sample of the chaotic attractor, with Girsanov-Radon-Nikodym corrections applied along each trajectory to preserve importance-sampling validity. A neural network trained by behavioral cloning replaces expensive online Monte Carlo rollouts with a single forward pass. On Lorenz-63 the per-particle controller reduces normalized RMSE by 77% relative to the uncontrolled particle filter at 70% lower computational cost than the oracle-nudged filter; the approach scales to Lorenz-96 with no architectural changes. The central argument is that importance-sampling correctness, not model complexity, is the primary constraint on learned particle filter controllers.

  2. IW
    Learning Particle-Wise Control for Nonlinear Data Assimilation: From Diffusion Models to Supervised Regression
    Sonal Bhatia
    Jan 2026
    Advisor: Prof. Ryne Beeson. Readers: Profs. Kolemen and Rowley. 86 pages.
    Abs Bib

    A systematic comparison of diffusion-based and regression-based learned control for particle filtering. Identifies a structural mismatch between generative models and importance sampling: diffusion models violate particle independence, lack importance-weight awareness, and are locally inconsistent on the chaotic attractor. A supervised regression framework maps particle state, observation, and time-to-observation to HJB-inspired nudging control; closed-loop experiments show strong deterministic control improves state correction but induces particle degeneracy without proposal correction.
