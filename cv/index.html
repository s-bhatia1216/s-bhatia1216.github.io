  • Senior Thesis, Department of Mechanical & Aerospace Engineering, Robotics
    Advisor: Prof. Ryne Beeson; Reader: Prof. Christine Allen-Blanchette · Aug 2025 to Apr 2026 · Princeton, NJ
    • Thesis: Multi-Particle Control for Nonlinear Data Assimilation in Chaotic Systems (189 pages, April 2026).
    • Developed an imitation-learning framework that amortizes the cost of optimal particle-filter nudging offline: a Feynman-Kac Monte Carlo oracle generates expert controls across a low-discrepancy sample of the chaotic attractor, with Girsanov-Radon-Nikodym corrections applied along each trajectory to preserve importance-sampling validity.
    • Trained a neural network via behavioral cloning to imitate the oracle’s per-particle control policy, replacing expensive online Monte Carlo rollouts with a single forward pass at deployment; evaluated four model variants: a per-particle regression baseline, two DeepSets ensemble-aware controllers with weight-aware pooling, and a residual architecture initialized from the per-particle checkpoint.
    • On Lorenz-63, the per-particle controller achieved a 77% reduction in normalized RMSE relative to the uncontrolled particle filter and a 4× improvement over the oracle-nudged particle filter, at 70% lower computational cost.
    • Demonstrated scaling to Lorenz-96 (K = 8 state variables) with no architectural changes, matching oracle accuracy while maintaining higher normalized effective sample size at 27% lower cost.
